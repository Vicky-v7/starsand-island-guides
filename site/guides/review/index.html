Starsand Island Review Roundup: Is It Worth Buying in 2026?

Last updated 2026-08-25 · Based on version 1.0 and community sources — items marked “TBC” are awaiting verification.

Starsand Island is genuinely divisive. On Steam it sits at "Mostly Positive" (78.5% of ~6,800 reviews at our August 25 snapshot), while critics average 68 on Metacritic and 73 on OpenCritic. Players love the art, the relaxed freedom, and the sheer amount of content; the loudest criticism targets a thin story, bugs, a rough multiplayer implementation, and a string of controversies around the studio. Here's both sides, as they actually appear in the data.

What Players and Critics Praise

  • Art and atmosphere: the most-echoed line — a cozy blend "reminiscent of Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing" with charming 3D anime visuals, capybaras and stray cats included. GAMINGbible went as far as "the very best of Animal Crossing, Stardew Valley, and The Sims, with Studio Ghibli flair."
  • Content volume and freedom: "a huge island full of things to do" — farming, fishing, ranching, building, exploration. Chinese reviewers repeatedly praise the 松弛感 (no-pressure pacing): grind if you want, wander if you don't.
  • Time-for-money value: a 284-hour reviewer's take — "for $40, I'm happy with that cost to time investment ratio." COGconnected called 1.0 "a massive leap forward from Early Access."

What the Negative Reviews Say

  • Thin story, lifeless NPCs: the most common substantive complaint across languages — "hollow narrative, underdeveloped relationships" (Game8), repetitive mid-game, awkward NPC dialogue.
  • Bugs, especially on console: Nintendo Life cited "ubiquitous bugs and performance issues" on Switch 2.
  • Multiplayer design: locked behind hours of single-player, and progress benefits the host — a top complaint in both English and Chinese reviews.
  • 1.0 felt like EA: several high-visibility reviews argue the full release "feels just as incomplete" as Early Access.
  • A cut romance option: the character Youfang was removed as a romanceable NPC shortly before 1.0, angering some Kickstarter-era supporters.

The Controversies, Stated Neutrally

Three separate issues fuel the harshest reviews. Each deserves precise framing:

  • Undisclosed AI-use allegations. Many negative reviews allege generative AI in text and art without Steam-page disclosure. This is a player allegation: the original February 2026 Reddit megathread itself noted there was no proof at the time, and no official confirmation or authoritative reporting has established it since — but by 1.0 it had become the single most frequent charge in negative reviews, including Chinese ones mocking the romance dialogue as AI-written.
  • Suspicious day-one positive reviews. When Early Access launched (Feb 11, 2026), a wave of glowing reviews appeared from accounts with minutes of playtime, some praising features not yet in the game; a YouTuber documented near-identical account patterns. Developer Seed Sparkle Lab publicly addressed it on Discord, saying the reviews were not its doing and asking whoever was behind them to stop (as reported by Game Rant; the original Discord post wasn't independently verified). Related allegations — that the "indie" studio has major-publisher backing, reportedly linked by players to Kingsoft/Seasun via trademark records — likewise remain player claims, not officially confirmed.
  • The April 2026 takedown. On April 10, 2026 the game was temporarily pulled from Steam after, in the developer's own words, a minigame "used core visual elements of a classic work without authorization." The studio removed the content, resubmitted, kept all saves intact, and compensated every player with the free Shining Star Outfit Set DLC. Press speculated the minigame resembled Tetris; the developer never named the work.

What's Next: Roadmap Reality Check

There is no official dated roadmap — the developer's stated position is "Version 1.0 is just the beginning. We'll continue bringing new content, improvements, and exciting updates." What's concretely on record: the first post-1.0 patch (v1.08.3916, Aug 20) shipped multiplayer fixes and ~15 single-player fixes two days after launch; PC gets patches first with consoles following platform certification; cross-platform play is being "looked into" with no date; a My Time at Portia character crossover has been announced, timing unconfirmed (TBC); and a costume design contest runs to September 7, 2026. All Kickstarter stretch goals (mounts, hoverboard, houseboat, red pandas and more) are reported implemented.

Should You Buy It?

Get it if you want a pretty, zero-pressure sandbox of farming, fishing, and building where the journey is the point — and you can shrug off jank. Skip it (or wait) if you're here for deep story, living NPCs, or friction-free co-op, if you're buying on Switch 2 before more patches land, or if the studio controversies above are dealbreakers for you. At $39.99 the hours-per-dollar math works for sandbox players; story-first players are the ones leaving the 50s-range scores.
